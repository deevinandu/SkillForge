pick_place@0.2.1 · round 1 · parent 0.2.0

The most common failure, finger-table collision (17/100 gate, 5/30 dev), happened on re-grasps: the grasp target's z came from noisy observations (std up to 0.015 m), so it could fall below the cube's resting height and drive the fingers into the table. v0.2.1 clamps the grasp z to at least the object's resting height. It also raises `settle_steps` from 4 to 10, because action latency (up to 6 steps) meant the lift could start before the fingers had closed, which caused slips and re-grasps. The placement check now averages 8 raw observations instead of reading one filtered sample, and the place target uses the planned grasp offset instead of the noisy offset measured at grip time; neither changed the dev score on its own. Dev success on the same 30 seeds went from 24/30 (80%: collision=5, timeout=1) to 27/30 (90%: no collisions; grasp_slip=1, timeout=1, wrong_placement=1), and the new version scored 49/60 (82%) on 60 dev seeds, a run with no matching baseline.

pick_place@0.2.2 · round 2 · parent 0.2.0

v0.2.1 targets the most common failure, finger-table collision on (re-)grasps: the grasp target came from one low-pass-filtered noisy observation (obs noise std up to 0.015 m), so the fingers were sometimes driven into the table or grasped the cube off-centre and it slipped. The skill now holds 6 steps before planning a grasp and aims at a running mean of raw object observations, restarting the mean if the object moves more than 4 cm. When that estimate puts the object resting on the table, it snaps the grasp z to the task's known rest height. The object-to-gripper offset used for placement and the post-release region check are also averaged over many raw samples, replacing single noisy readings. Dev success on the 30 seeds went from 80% (24/30: collision=5, timeout=1) to 87% (26/30: grasp_slip=2, timeout=2, no collisions); on 100 dev seeds it is 79% with only 1 collision, and the remaining failures are mostly grasp slips (12) and timeouts (7), which this change does not address (the 0.2.0 baseline was not run on 100 dev seeds).

pick_place@0.2.3 · round 3 · parent 0.2.0

v0.2.1 targets the most common failure, finger-table collision, which happened on re-picks after a release: noisy observations (std up to 0.015 m) gave a bad carry offset, so the cube landed off-centre or a single noisy verify reading flagged it as outside the 3 cm bin, and the re-grasp then took its z from a noisy reading and drove the fingers into the table. The skill now keeps a running mean of the raw object position while the object sits still and uses it for all grasp and verify targets, with z pinned to the known resting half-height when the cube is on the table or bin floor. While carrying, it averages the observed object-to-EE offset and uses that average for the place target instead of the single offset taken at grasp time. It also holds for 6 steps after retreating so the verify check runs on an averaged estimate. Dev success went from 24/30 (80%, collision=5, timeout=1) to 29/30 (97%) on the same 30 seeds, and 95/100 on 100 dev seeds with no collisions; the remaining failures are 4 timeouts from place/re-pick loops and 1 grasp slip.

pick_place@0.2.4 · round 4 · parent 0.2.3

v0.2.4 replaces the hold monitor with `SmoothedHoldMonitor`, which measures carry drift on an EMA of the raw object-to-EE offset, compared against a reference averaged over the first 8 carried samples, instead of on single raw noisy samples. It targets the most common failure, the "timeout: still holding" pattern (grasp, release, grasp, release, grasp; 7/100 gate, 4/100 dev) and the related grasp_slip cases. Diagnostics run inside the skill showed these were false `grasp_slip:object_drifting(0.03-0.04 m)` aborts right after lift: with observation noise up to 0.015 m per axis, single samples break the 0.03 m tolerance three steps in a row often enough to drop a good grasp and loop re-picks until time runs out. Width-collapse detection and a hard 0.05 m loss threshold on the smoothed offset are kept, so real losses are still caught within a few steps. Dev success went from 95/100 (29/30 on the default 30 seeds) to 100/100 (30/30); this is measured in sim only and the gate seeds are not yet re-evaluated.
